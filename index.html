Fig. 4 A check-in desk for a summit

  1. Baseline. A glowing orb and identical cards. The form sat below the fold, and the scores were hard to read from across a table.
  2. Intervention. A registration-desk scoreboard: 50 seats fill in team colors in arrival order. One-tap team tiles, plus staff tools for undoing a mistake.
  3. Result. The original logic and its self-test were kept, and a scripted run of 23 check-ins passed at phone and desktop widths. An independent review is still pending.

Method

Each revision ran the same protocol, in this order.

  1. Freeze the baseline. The graded version is tagged in git before anything changes, so panel a is always recoverable.
  2. Write the brief. Who uses the page, what they need from it, and which facts must stay true.
  3. Replace filler with sources. Numbers come from the primary document, cited down to the page.
  4. Measure. Rendered and checked in a real browser at 320, 390 and 1440 px, with reduced motion on and off, and no console errors.
  5. Review independently. A second model audits the work read-only, and I apply its fixes or record why not.

These revisions were built with AI assistance (Claude Code, with Codex as reviewer). The judgment calls, the sources and what shipped are mine to answer for.
